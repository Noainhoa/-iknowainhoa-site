## What I wish I’d tell you on the street

Like all life stories has many stories but my takes now, and what I want all those of you that I will assault on the street because I want to say hi are:

### ALWAYS keep moving… WHY?

Keeping a dog moving stops adrenaline from building up by burning off nervous energy, preventing fixation on a trigger, and stopping them from reacting.

When a dog stands still and stares at something “scary” for them, or simply unknown, their body pumps out stress chemicals. Keeping them walking helps them stay calm.

Dogs get scared from a distance because they cannot smell the object to identify it, and their distance vision is actually quite blurry. To a dog, a far-away shape is just a mysterious, blurry monster until they get close enough to sniff it.

**STORY:** Gringo will have a muntjac in front of him but he can not see it. He can however smell the scent from where the muntjac has walked, so he will pick on that and run in zigzags wherever the muntjac was, getting closer but totally missing the muntjac right 4 m in front of him.

### Smelling is “Seeing”

**Nose comes first:** A dog’s brain uses smell to truly understand the world. Scientists found that a dog’s nose is wired directly into their brain’s visual screen. [1, 2, 3]

**No smell, no trust:** If a trigger is 50 feet away, the wind isn’t carrying its scent. Because the dog cannot smell it, they cannot identify if it is a friend or an enemy. [1, 2]

**Mystery equals danger:** In the animal world, if you don’t know what something is, you assume it wants to eat you.

When you keep your dog moving, they have the time to look at another dog safely without getting flooded with stress chemicals. Because you are right there to show them everything is okay, they stay calm.

You can walk them in a big circle or side-by-side, but never let them walk straight at each other face-to-face.

### Face-to-face vs side-by-side

Try this at home with a friend to feel the difference: standing directly face-to-face instantly feels tense and high-energy because it feels dangerous. Standing shoulder-to-shoulder, both looking forward, feels completely safe and low-energy. This proves that a head-on approach triggers tension, while a side-by-side setup keeps everyone safe and calm.

Try this with any dog: meeting head-on creates a massive explosion of tense energy, while walking side-by-side or behind each other keeps the energy completely calm.
